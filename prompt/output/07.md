## 1. The finding that changes the framing

The brief assumes the gap is "no defined remedy." It is worse than that: **the platform cannot execute
any remedy at all, and one path is actively deadlocked.**

A photographer accepting an assignment flips the booking to `in_progress`
([`AssignedBookingController.php:172`](../../app/Http/Controllers/StudioPhotographer/AssignedBookingController.php#L172)).
`in_progress` is the exact status that blocks the owner from assigning a replacement
([`BookingController.php:288`](../../app/Http/Controllers/StudioOwner/BookingController.php#L288)) **and**
from removing the dead assignment
([`BookingController.php:816`](../../app/Http/Controllers/StudioOwner/BookingController.php#L816)).

So a photographer who accepts and then cancels leaves a paid, scheduled booking `in_progress` with zero
active photographers and the owner locked out of both recoveries. The guards exist to protect a shoot
genuinely under way; they cannot tell that apart from a shoot nobody is going to. The only escape is
cancelling the whole booking on a client who did nothing wrong.

Cancellation itself is silent — it writes `status`, `cancelled_at`, and `cancellation_reason` on the
assignment row and stops
([`AssignedBookingController.php:275`](../../app/Http/Controllers/StudioPhotographer/AssignedBookingController.php#L275)).
No booking change, no owner notification, no client notification, no payment action.

## 2. Why no remedy is currently available

| Remedy | Blocker |
|---|---|
| Substitute photographer | The `in_progress` deadlock above |
| Reschedule | No controller ever changes `event_date`/`start_time`/`end_time` after creation |
| Refund | **Neither `PaymongoService` nor `StripeService` has a refund call.** `refund_pending` (set by roadmap 2.5) is read by nothing, has no badge mapping, no queue; `PAYMENT_REFUNDED` is declared and never assigned; the freelancer-side handler is a `Log::info` stub |
| Credit | No credit or wallet concept exists — `tbl_client_budget` is a spending planner, not stored value |
| Accountability | Cancellation reasons are stored and read nowhere |

Refunds also have a hidden prerequisite: `SystemRevenueModel::markAsRefunded()` exists and no booking
path calls it, so returning money today would leave platform revenue overstated and the owner income
report (roadmap 2.2) wrong.

## 3. The nine options

| | Client disruption | Session proceeds | Money moves | Build | Gated by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** Substitute, same slot | Low | **Yes** | No | **S** | D1, D2 |
| **B** Reschedule, same photographer | Medium | Yes, later | No | M | D1 |
| **C** Reschedule + replacement | High | Yes, later | No | M | D1, D2 |
| **D** Full refund | High | **No** | Out | **L** | D3, D4 |
| **E** Partial refund | High | No | Out, partly | L | D3, D4, D6 |
| **F** Booking credit | Medium | Not this one | Held as liability | **XL** | D3, D5 |
| **G** Manual escalation + SLA | Low–High | Maybe | No | **S** | none |
| **H** Widen the substitution pool | Low | **Yes** | Studio margin only | M | D8 |
| **I** Value-gap refund on downgrade | Low | Yes | Out, partly | S after D | D4, D8 |

Not mutually exclusive — a real policy is a decision tree over several. **A, G, and H are the ones that
save the session**; A reuses
[`PhotographerAvailabilityService::getAvailabilityMapForBooking()`](../../app/Services/PhotographerAvailabilityService.php#L33)
so it needs no new matching logic. **D is the expensive floor** when substitution fails. **I stops
substitution quietly shortchanging the client.** **F is a separate project** wearing a remedy's clothing.

## 4. The case that drives the design: the date cannot move

The defining scenario is narrower than the brief's framing — the client has prepared for months, the
event happens **on that day**, and the photographer cancels with days or hours to go. There, most of the
option set evaporates: reschedule is inapplicable, credit offers a future shoot for a once-only
occasion, and a refund on the morning does not get anyone photographed.

**Only substitution helps**, which reduces the design question to *how wide the net goes and how fast*:
studio roster → studio off-duty staff on overtime → **platform freelancers** → partner studio → nobody
found. Step 3 is the unused asset — the platform already carries freelancers with categories and
schedules that have never been connected to studio bookings.

Two further consequences, both documented in §5 of the contingency doc:

- **Notice changes what the client is owed.** Weeks out they get choice; hours out there is no time to
  ask, and their protection shifts from choice to money (Option I plus compensation). The D2
  acknowledgment window therefore cannot be a fixed number.
- **The ladder can terminate in failure**, and that outcome should be designed rather than improvised:
  full refund, compensation on top, and a record against the photographer.

Prevention outranks every remedy here, because by event morning all the options are bad. Two cheap
levers: restrict late self-cancellation (today it is one click at any moment until on-site), and allow a
named backup on high-value bookings.

## 5. Recommended build set

Offered as a **recommendation, not a decision** — D1–D9 remain open, per the brief's constraint that
options be evaluated before a policy is finalised. Nine methods in three tiers:

| Tier | Items | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — required (4)** | 9.1 cascade+unlock · 9.2 notify · 9.3 substitution · 9.5 refund | 9.1 and 9.2 are gated by nothing and can start now. 9.5 is the one real project. Stop here and no paid client is stranded silently again |
| **2 — high value (3)** | 9.8 freelancer pool · 9.9 value-gap refund · 9.7 cancellation record | 9.8 is the best return per unit of work |
| **3 — prevention (2)** | 9.10 late-cancellation restriction · 9.11 backup photographer | Cheapest items, largest effect |
| **not recommended (2)** | 9.6 credit ledger · 9.4 reschedule | Credit creates a standing liability and reads as coercive when the provider cancelled; reschedule only helps when the date can move, which was never the urgent case |

## 6. Decisions required before implementation

D1 who chooses the remedy (owner, client, or owner-proposes/client-accepts) · D2 may the client reject a
substitute, and inside what window · D3 automated gateway refunds or a manual finance queue · D4 who
absorbs the platform fee and any studio payout already made · D5 is credit ever offered *instead of*
cash · D6 are partial refunds ever acceptable when the **provider** cancelled · D7 what consequence
attaches to a cancelling photographer · D8 how wide the substitution net goes · D9 should late
cancellation be restricted, and from what point.

**D1, D2, D8, D9 gate the options that actually save the session.** D3, D4, D6 gate money movement. D5
can be answered "no" with no loss of coverage.

Regardless of how these land, three pieces are needed under every option and can start now: cascade the
assignment cancellation so the state stops lying and recovery unblocks (9.1), notify the owner and client
(9.2), and optionally the backup-photographer mechanism (9.11).

## 8. Related defects surfaced, not fixed

1. [`MyBookingsController.php:279`](../../app/Http/Controllers/Client/MyBookingsController.php#L279) —
   client cancellation overwrites `payment_status` with `'cancelled'` even on a fully paid booking,
   destroying the record that money was received. It will collide with any refund state Phase 9 adds.
2. `tbl_bookings.payment_status` is an unconstrained string, not an enum — new states cost nothing to
   write and nothing renders them. `refund_pending` is the existing proof.
3. Freelancer bookings are a parallel unanalysed case: the freelancer *is* the photographer, so
   substitution is impossible and the brief collapses to reschedule/refund/credit.
4. Multi-photographer bookings are unmodelled — one of four cancelling is a partial-staffing problem,
   and `allPhotographersCompleted()` assumes every assignment eventually completes.

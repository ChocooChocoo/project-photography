# Objective

## Refine and Implement Core Studio Management System Requirements

---

## Role

You are a systems analyst and software requirements specialist. Approach this objective with the mindset, vocabulary, and priorities of a professional responsible for translating evaluator feedback into clear, actionable functional and security requirements.

---

## Description

Refine and formalize the discussed requirements for a studio management platform covering registration, verification, administration, employee management, access control, attendance, and client booking. The system should provide secure authentication, clearly defined user roles, structured business permit verification, and efficient administrative review workflows. It should also improve usability through guided onboarding, configurable business settings, automated notifications, and complete employee account provisioning. All requirements must be expressed clearly enough to guide system design, development, testing, and evaluation.

---

## Primary Objective

Translate the evaluator’s feedback into a clear and actionable set of system requirements for improving the security, verification, administration, user management, attendance, and client-facing functions of the studio management platform.

---

## Secondary Objectives

* Strengthen authentication and account security for administrators, business owners, and employees.
* Ensure business permits are verified before studio configuration and operational access are granted.
* Improve the efficiency and traceability of studio application approval, rejection, and resubmission workflows.
* Enhance onboarding and form usability for business owners, employees, administrators, and clients.
* Support flexible roles and granular permissions through dynamic Role-Based Access Control.
* Improve attendance integrity through configurable schedules, live photo capture, and geolocation verification.
* Expand client-facing booking and discovery features, including favorites and visible package images.
* Allow business owners to define their own pricing, down-payment, and discount rules.
* Ensure all deletion processes preserve records through soft deletion or archiving.

---

## Success Criteria

* Registration captures complete owner, studio, location, service, pricing, and role information.
* Administrative accounts require email-based OTP verification during login.
* Administrators use a dedicated login interface separate from the shared user login page.
* Users cannot fully access or configure the studio module until their business permit has been verified.
* Business permit records include an expiration date and support re-verification after expiration.
* Administrators can review submitted documents, select standardized rejection reasons, and track document resubmission counts.
* New users receive an onboarding tutorial with “Next” and “Skip” controls.
* Approved business owners receive an automated email notification.
* Newly created employees receive secure temporary credentials and are required to change their password during their first login.
* The RBAC module supports combined roles and category-level “Select All” permission controls.
* All deletion actions use soft deletion or archiving rather than permanent deletion.
* Work schedules support different start and end times for each day.
* Time-in and time-out transactions require live photo capture and geolocation-radius validation.
* Clients can save studios or companies as favorites and view images associated with packages and galleries.
* Discounts are configured by business owners rather than imposed through hardcoded system rules.

---

## Out of Scope

* Hardcoded or mandatory system-level discounts, including an automatic 5% discount for full payments.
* Permanent hard deletion of system records.

---

## Context & Dependencies

* Business permit verification is a prerequisite for full access to the studio configuration module.
* Email delivery is required for OTP codes, employee credentials, temporary passwords, and approval notifications.
* Camera access is required for live attendance photo capture.
* Location services are required to validate whether employees are within the permitted geolocation radius during time-in and time-out.
* Permit expiration tracking depends on capturing and monitoring each permit’s validity date.
* Employee access depends on successful account creation, credential delivery, and completion of the mandatory first-login password change.
* Administrative approval depends on the availability of submitted business documents in the built-in review interface.

---

## Stakeholders

* Business owners who register studios, configure services, manage pricing, and define discounts.
* Administrators who authenticate securely and review, approve, reject, or request resubmission of studio applications.
* Human Resources personnel who manage employee records, schedules, roles, and attendance.
* Marketing managers who may require role-specific access to promotional or studio-related functions.
* Finance personnel who may share combined permissions with other roles.
* Employees who receive accounts, credentials, schedules, and attendance access.
* Clients who browse studios, view packages, save favorites, and make bookings.
* Evaluators who assess whether the system satisfies the identified functional, security, and usability requirements.

## Supporting Tasks

### Registration and Studio Creation

* Collect complete business owner information during account registration.
* Allow users to specify the type of studio being registered.
* Capture studio locations and related business details.
* Require users to identify their organizational role, such as Business Owner, Human Resources Manager, or Marketing Manager.
* Add minimum and maximum price fields to represent a complete pricing range.
* Provide a configurable option that allows business owners to enable or disable down-payment requirements.
* Add an “Others” option to service categories for services not included in the predefined list.
* Expand supported social media links to include platforms such as LinkedIn.

### Authentication and Account Security

* Require email-based OTP verification when users log in, with particular emphasis on administrative accounts.
* Create a dedicated administrator login page instead of using the same login interface provided to standard users.
* Protect sensitive administrative credentials through stronger authentication controls.
* Send secure access credentials or OTP instructions to newly created employee accounts.
* Automatically generate and email a temporary password when an employee account is created.
* Require employees to replace the temporary password during their first successful login.

### Business Permit Verification

* Require business permit verification before allowing users to fully access or configure the studio module.
* Add a permit expiration date field to each business permit record.
* Track permit validity based on the recorded expiration date.
* Prompt or require business owners to complete re-verification when a permit expires.

### Administrative Approval

* Provide an administrative panel that lists pending studio applications.
* Allow administrators to inspect uploaded permits and supporting documents through a built-in document viewer.
* Provide a drop-down list containing standardized rejection reasons.
* Allow administrators to approve or reject applications after reviewing the submitted documents.
* Record the number of times an applicant has resubmitted or re-uploaded documents.
* Send an automated email to the business owner after the application has been verified and approved.

### User Experience and Onboarding

* Display an onboarding tutorial or guided wizard for newly registered users.
* Include “Next” and “Skip” controls in the onboarding experience.
* Explain important fields, buttons, and system functions through the onboarding flow.
* Ensure pricing forms clearly distinguish between starting prices and maximum prices.
* Clearly present down-payment settings as configurable business options.

### Employee Profile Management

* Add a suffix field to employee and user name forms for values such as “Jr.” or “III.”
* Ensure newly created employee profiles are linked to valid system accounts.
* Deliver account credentials securely through email.
* Enforce a first-login password-change requirement for temporary passwords.

### Role-Based Access Control

* Support dynamic roles that combine permissions from multiple functional areas.
* Allow a single account to hold combined responsibilities, such as Human Resources and Finance.
* Provide granular permission assignment by module or category.
* Add a “Select All” checkbox or toggle within each permission category.
* Ensure category-level selection reduces the need to assign permissions individually.

### Record Deletion and Archiving

* Implement all deletion functions as soft-delete operations.
* Preserve deleted records in an archived or inactive state.
* Prevent ordinary deletion actions from permanently removing records from the system.

### Attendance and Schedule Management

* Allow administrators or authorized managers to configure different work start and end times for each day.
* Support schedule variations such as weekend shifts and shortened Saturday schedules.
* Require employees to capture a live photograph using the device camera during time-in.
* Require another live photograph during time-out.
* Validate each attendance transaction against the configured geolocation radius.
* Use photo capture and location verification together to reduce proxy attendance or buddy punching.

### Client Portal and Booking

* Allow clients to bookmark or mark studios and companies as favorites.
* Provide a convenient way for clients to revisit their saved studios or companies.
* Display supporting images in package previews.
* Ensure gallery images are clearly visible within client-facing studio listings.
* Present relevant visual content while clients browse packages and booking options.

### Pricing and Discount Management

* Remove any hardcoded discount automatically applied by the system.
* Eliminate mandatory discounts such as an automatic 5% reduction for full payment.
* Allow each business owner to decide whether discounts will be offered.
* Allow business owners to define and manage their own discount rules.

## Detailed Breakdown

### Registration and Role Specification

The registration process must guide users through account and studio creation while collecting complete owner, studio, location, service, and pricing information. The setup flow must explicitly ask users to identify their role within the organization instead of treating all registrants as equivalent account holders. Role choices may include Business Owner, Human Resources Manager, or Marketing Manager based on the responsibilities identified during registration. Pricing must support both a starting value and an ending or maximum value, while down-payment requirements must remain configurable by the business owner.

### Administrative Authentication

Administrative access must be separated from the standard shared login process through a dedicated administrator login page. Administrative accounts must use email-based OTP verification to reduce the risk associated with compromised or reused credentials. The authentication workflow must protect access to sensitive functions such as application review, verification, user management, and permission administration.

### Permit-Gated Studio Access

Business permit verification must occur before a newly registered business can fully access or configure its studio module. Submitted permits must include an expiration date so the system can determine whether the document remains valid. Once a permit expires, the system must identify the business as requiring re-verification before continued verified operation.

### Application Review and Resubmission Tracking

Administrators must be able to review pending studio applications and inspect uploaded documents through a built-in viewer. When rejecting an application, the administrator must be able to select from a standardized list of rejection reasons to improve consistency and review efficiency. The system must record how many times an applicant has resubmitted or replaced documents, allowing administrators to monitor repeated submissions. After approval, the system must automatically notify the business owner by email.

### Guided User Onboarding

New users must receive an onboarding pop-up, tutorial, or wizard that introduces important fields, controls, and navigation elements. The tutorial must provide “Next” and “Skip” options so users can either complete the guidance sequentially or dismiss it. The onboarding process should focus on helping users understand how to interact with the available modules and configuration fields.

### Secure Employee Account Provisioning

When an employee account is created, the system must automatically generate temporary login credentials and deliver them through email. The temporary password must not serve as a permanent credential. During the employee’s first successful login, the system must require the employee to create a new password before gaining normal access.

### Dynamic and Combined Roles

The RBAC module must support roles composed of permissions from multiple functional areas. A user may therefore be assigned a combined role, such as Human Resources and Finance, without requiring separate accounts. Permission assignment must include a category-level “Select All” control so administrators can grant all permissions within a category efficiently while retaining the ability to configure individual permissions.

### Soft Deletion Policy

Every deletion action in the system must preserve the affected record through soft deletion or archiving. Records marked as deleted must become inactive or hidden from normal operational views without being permanently removed. This requirement applies across all modules where users or administrators can delete data.

### Configurable Daily Schedules

Attendance schedules must support separate start and end times for each day rather than relying on one fixed schedule for the entire week. This configuration must accommodate varying shifts, including weekend work and different Saturday hours. Authorized users must be able to define these daily schedule values according to operational requirements.

### Photo and Location Attendance Validation

Employees must capture a live photograph through the device camera whenever they record a time-in or time-out transaction. The system must also confirm that the employee is within the permitted geolocation radius. Both controls must be applied together to strengthen attendance verification and reduce buddy punching or proxy attendance.

### Client Favorites and Image Presentation

The client portal must allow users to bookmark or favorite preferred studios or companies for faster future access. Client-facing package previews and galleries must display their associated images clearly. Images should remain visible while clients evaluate studio offerings, packages, and booking options.

### Owner-Controlled Commercial Rules

Pricing and discount rules must remain under the control of the business owner. The system must not automatically impose a discount for full payment or any other payment condition. Business owners must be able to decide whether discounts are available and define the applicable discount rules themselves.
